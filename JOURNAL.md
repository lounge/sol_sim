# Journal

The milestone-by-milestone record of this project: what was built, the bugs hit on the way, and the lessons each one taught. Entries are chronological, newest at the bottom, and preserved as written — this is the history. `ROADMAP.md` holds the roadmap.

- **learnopengl exercises** through the shaders chapter are done (`opengl/learn/1_window`, `opengl/learn/2_triangle`, `opengl/learn/3_shaders`).
- **Rendering**: the sim renders a procedurally generated circle mesh (TRIANGLE_FAN), drawn once per body at any position/size via `offset`/`scale`/`aspect` uniforms.
- **Physics**: working N-body gravity — `Body` struct, pairwise Newton, velocity Verlet since milestone 16 (originally semi-implicit Euler with all kicks applied before the position pass — an ordering that matters; see the milestone 16 bullet). Integration is substepped in fixed DT gulps (see milestone 13 bullet).
- **Sun, Earth, and Moon** in hierarchical orbits: velocities composed inside-out (moon.vel = earth.vel + local circular speed), total momentum zeroed via the Sun, Moon kept inside Earth's Hill radius.
- **Unit scaling** is done: G=1 with distances in AU and masses in solar masses (Sun 1.0, Earth 3.003e-6, Moon 3.69e-8; Moon orbit 2.57e-3 AU), so real mass/distance ratios hold without overflowing float precision. Physics state (`Body` pos/vel/mass) is f64, converted to f32 only at the shader-uniform boundary.
- **2D camera** is done (`camera.odin`): a `Camera` (center + half_extent, tracked body index) replaces the hardcoded half-extent; `calc_ndc` in `render.odin` does the world→NDC transform in f64 before the f32 uniform boundary. Scroll wheel zooms multiplicatively (`half_extent *= pow(0.9, yoffset)`); tracking was originally number keys, since replaced by click-to-track (see milestone 12 bullet); on track change the zoom resets to `body.radius * VIEW_SCALE` so every tracked body appears the same on-screen size. The camera was originally a package-level global because GLFW callbacks are `proc "c"` and can't capture locals — since replaced by `State` behind the window user pointer (see the milestone 17 State refactor bullet); `camera_zoom` is `proc "contextless"`.

- **Real body sizes** are done: `Body.radius` (renamed from `size`) holds real radii in AU (sun 4.654e-3, earth 4.259e-5, moon 1.161e-5). Sub-pixel bodies are clamped in screen space: `calc_ndc_scale` in `render.odin` returns `max(MIN_MARKER_PX / (height/2), radius / half_extent)`, so every body renders at least `MIN_MARKER_PX` (4) pixels of radius at any zoom or window size.

- **Orbit trails** are done: each body has a `Trail` (`render.odin`) — a fixed `[TRAIL_CAP][2]f64` ring buffer with a per-body logical `cap` computed in `main` from the orbital period (`T = 2πr/v`, Kepler's third law) as ~85% of one orbit, so trails don't quite close. Points are recorded once per frame *relative to a parent body* (`parent` index: sun → world frame, earth → sun, moon → earth) and re-anchored to the parent's current position at draw time, so the moon's trail is a clean arc riding along with the earth. Drawing unrolls each ring oldest→newest (oldest is `head` once `count == cap`, else 0) through `calc_ndc_offset` in f64 into an f32 scratch buffer, uploads into one shared `DYNAMIC_DRAW` VBO via `gl.BufferSubData`, and draws `LINE_STRIP` with `offset = 0, scale = 1`.

- **Spec-driven setup** (`system.odin`): bodies and trails are built by `create_system` from a declarative `BodySpec` table (mass, radius, ecc, semi_major_axis, parent index, name). Positions/velocities are composed inside-out from the parent's state (specs must be ordered parent-before-child, asserted), trail caps derive from each orbital period (`TRAIL_FRACTION` of one orbit), the root's cap is the longest child's, and total momentum is zeroed via the root. Adding a planet = one spec line.

- **Inner planets** (milestone 10) are done: Mercury, Venus, Mars in the spec table in solar order (keys 1–6 track Sun→Mars; Moon's parent index is 3). `TRAIL_CAP` is 12,800 to fit Mars's 1.88-year period.

- **Real eccentricities** (milestone 11) are done: each body starts at perihelion (`r = a(1−e)`, velocity tangential there) with speed from the vis-viva equation `v = √(GM(2/r − 1/a))`, where M is the parent's mass. Trail caps use Kepler's third law proper, `T = 2π√(a³/GM)` — independent of `e`, so periods and caps match the circular era. The Sun sits at a focus (Mercury's trail is visibly off-center) and per-frame trail-point spacing shows Kepler's second law.

- **Outer planets** (milestone 12) are done: Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, Pluto in the spec table. Pluto has `start_at_aphelion = true` (`r = a(1+e)`, vis-viva works at any r) — started at perihelion it would spawn ~0.07 AU from Neptune, where Neptune's pull is ~10× the Sun's, and get slingshotted. Their perihelion regions coincide because every body starts on the +x axis. Real semi-major axes give the sim a near-3:2 Neptune–Pluto period ratio (1.504), so the crossing orbits stay ≥19 AU apart (verified headless over a full 248-yr Pluto orbit) — though the initial phases aren't a true librating resonance, so ultra-long runs could still scatter Pluto.
- **Trail strides** (the milestone-12 wall): each trail has a `stride` in *physics steps* (`ceil(TRAIL_FRACTION × steps_per_orbit / TRAIL_CAP)`), so `cap × stride` spans ~95% of the orbit inside the fixed 12,800-point ring regardless of period. `record_trail` runs once per physics step *inside* the substep loop (positions mid-frame only exist there — recording per frame at high sim speed aliases fast orbits into star polygons) and uses a step accumulator: `frame_count += 1`, record on crossing `stride`, then `frame_count %= stride`. The Sun's trail copies cap/stride from its most massive child (Jupiter) so its barycentric wobble — a loop ~2 solar radii wide, since the Sun–Jupiter barycenter is outside the photosphere — reads cleanly. Momentum zeroing is now properly barycentric: `v_cm = Σmv/Σm` subtracted from every body.
- **Interaction rework** (with milestone 12): polled `process_input` is gone; all input is GLFW callbacks. Click-to-track: the mouse callback stores `pending_click: Maybe([2]f64)` — originally on the camera, now in `Input` (see the milestone 17 State refactor bullet) — because callbacks are `proc "c"` and can't see `bodies` (request now, apply later); `camera_update` consumes it, picking the nearest body whose *screen-space* distance is within `max(marker_px, PICK_RADIUS_PX)` via `calc_screen_pos` (forward world→pixel transform in `render.odin`; hit-testing in pixels is what makes sub-pixel bodies clickable at their clamped marker size). Empty-space click → free camera (`tracked_body = -1`). Key callback: Escape quits; left/right arrows halve/double `sim_speed` (a runtime `int` replacing the `STEPS_PER_FRAME` constant; DT is untouched — accuracy is per-step, speed is step count; `PRESS|REPEAT` gives tap-or-hold for free).

- **Fixed timestep** (milestone 13, "Fix Your Timestep") is done: the per-frame `sim_speed`-step loop is replaced by a wall-clock accumulator. `T_UNIT_SECONDS` (1 yr/2π ≈ 5.023e6 s, the G=1/AU/solar-mass time unit) anchors sim time to real time: each frame adds `frame_time × sim_speed / T_UNIT_SECONDS` (in sim time units) to an accumulator drained in gulps of DT, so `sim_speed` is now literally sim-seconds per real second (1 = real time; default 200,000 ≈ 2.3 days/sec restores the old feel) and identical on any display/frame rate. DT is untouched — accuracy stays per-step. `frame_time` is clamped to 0.1 s so stalls (macOS title-bar drag blocks `PollEvents`) can't dump a huge step debt in one frame; sim time falls behind wall time under stress instead. `accumulator`/`last_time` live outside the frame loop (the leftover fraction must pool across frames); vsync is on (`SwapInterval(1)`).

- **Per-body shaders/colors** (milestone 14) are done: the single shader pair is split into two programs — `body.vert/frag.glsl` (flat `color` uniform) and `trail.vert/frag.glsl` (color plus fade). Uniforms are per-program state, so each program's shared uniforms are set after its own `UseProgram` in the frame loop. Colors live in `colors.odin` as three swappable `Color_Set` palettes (albedo / realistic / vibrant), selected by the package-level `palette := realistic.body` (declared in `main.odin`); `BodySpec` and `Body` carry `color: [3]f32` (f32 is fine — color exists only for the uniform boundary). Trail fade rides on the existing ring unroll: because points are copied oldest→newest into the scratch buffer, `gl_VertexID` equals point age, so the trail vertex shader computes `t_alpha = gl_VertexID / count` (a `trail.count` uniform — NOT the scratch array's constant length) and the fragment shader uses it as alpha, with blending (`SRC_ALPHA`, `ONE_MINUS_SRC_ALPHA`) enabled at init. Note the coupling: the fade is correct only while trails are drawn via that copy-unroll; drawing the ring in place would break it and require a real per-vertex attribute. Alongside: `sim_speed` is clamped to `MAX_SIM_SPEED` (15 sim-years/sec), `T_UNIT_SECONDS` is derived as `SECONDS_IN_YEAR / (2 * math.PI)` (parenthesization matters — `/ 2 * math.PI` once shipped a π²≈9.87× slowdown), and the title shows days/sec, years/sec, and the raw `sim_speed`.

- **Render interpolation** (the second half of "Fix Your Timestep") is done: at very low `sim_speed` the sim advances in discrete DT gulps (one physics step ≈ 502 sim-seconds, so at 1× one step per ~8.4 real minutes), which used to render as freeze-then-lurch. Now `physics_step` copies `pos → prev_pos` for every body at the top of each step, and after the drain loop `alpha := accumulator / DT` (the leftover fraction, always in [0,1)) feeds `render_pos` in `render.odin` — `prev_pos + (pos − prev_pos) × alpha` — a display-only lerp used everywhere a position reaches the screen: `draw_bodies`, the tracked-camera center and click-picking in `camera_update`, and the trail parent re-anchor. The physics never sees `alpha`; the render shows the state up to one DT in the past. Trails live on that same rendered timeline: `record_trail` stores `prev_pos` (relative to the parent's `prev_pos`), so no recorded point is ever ahead of the drawn body, and one synthetic vertex — the body's `render_pos` — is appended to each strip at draw time (never recorded; buffers sized `TRAIL_CAP + 1`, fade/upload/draw use `trail.count + 1`) as a pure forward extension that terminates the strip exactly on the body. (Recording `pos` — step-time "present" — instead made the tip overshoot the interpolated body and double back: invisible at planet scale, ~20 px at Io scale.)

- **Jupiter's moons** (milestone 15) are done: Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto in the spec table with `parent = 6` (Jupiter), all sharing `palette.Moon`. They sit mid-list between Jupiter and Saturn — safe *here* because Jupiter (6) precedes them and everything after them parents to the Sun (0), but it shifted the outer-planet indices: Saturn is now 11, Uranus 12, Neptune 13, Pluto 14. Future specs must count against the current list (Titan would need `parent = 11`). Io runs ~304 steps/orbit (fastest in the sim); above a few sim-days/sec the Galilean moons temporally alias — tens of orbits per frame reads as near-standstill wobble (wagon-wheel effect) while the trail still draws the full ellipse.

- **Velocity Verlet** (milestone 16) is done — with a plot twist. `physics_step` is kick–drift–kick: half-kick from stored `Body.accel`, drift, one `compute_accels` pass, half-kick. `compute_accels` zeroes every accel then accumulates `±direction · G·m_other/d²` per pair (own mass cancels — no force/mass round trip); all geometry lives there, kicks are uniform `vel += accel * (dt/2)`. `create_system` ends with a priming `compute_accels` call — without it the first half-kick is a silent no-op. The expected precession collapse never happened, and the headless measurement (Jupiter–Io two-body, e=0.3, ~304 steps/orbit) explains why: kick-all-then-drift-all Euler is *exactly conjugate to leapfrog* — n Euler steps equal a half-kick, n Verlet steps, and an inverse half-kick, because adjacent half-kicks at the same positions merge — so its long-term orbit geometry was already second-order. Measured: −0.053°/orbit precession under both integrators, both improving ~4× per DT halving. What Verlet actually bought: pos/vel synchronized at the same instant, so the *energy oscillation* collapsed ~80× (1.8e-2 → 2.3e-4 relative) and scales as h² where Euler's scaled as h. The true non-symplectic failure mode was verified with a temporary explicit Euler (drift-before-kick — the entire difference is two swapped loop lines): it pumps ~3% energy per orbit into Io, which escapes Jupiter within ~30 orbits.

- **Velocity burns** (milestone 17, first half) are done: up/down arrows apply prograde/retrograde burns to the tracked body — `vel *= 1 + pending_vel/100`, a *fractional* scaling (equivalent to Δv along the velocity direction) so one press means the same thing for Io as for Pluto. `pending_vel` is an int accumulator in `Input`: the key callback adds ±1 per press (accumulation matters when a `PollEvents` stall flushes queued repeats into one frame), and `apply_pending_vel` (`physics.odin`) consumes it once per frame *outside* the drain loop — per-step application would tie burn strength to `sim_speed`. Consume-and-reset is load-bearing: the first version forgot the reset and applied 1% *per frame*, compounding ~1.8×/sec (1.01⁶⁰). Clamped at −99 so the factor stays positive (at −100 one press zeroes the velocity; below, it reverses the orbit); pending edits are discarded when no body is tracked. Burns need no `compute_accels` refresh — acceleration depends on positions and masses, never velocities. Mass edits do (see next bullet).

- **State refactor** (with milestone 17): the package-level globals are gone. `State` (`state.odin`: `sim_speed`, `Input`, `Camera`) is a local of `main`, registered once via `glfw.SetWindowUserPointer`; callbacks reach it through `get_state`, which casts the window's one `rawptr` slot to `^State`. The lesson that forced this shape: GLFW has exactly *one* user-pointer slot per window, and the cast is a raw reinterpretation with zero checking — two getters casting the same slot to different types can't both be right (the interim version registered `&input_state` and cast it to `^Camera`; zoom silently scribbled on `Input`'s bytes). So the slot points at one struct bundling everything, initialized *before* callbacks can fire (a zero camera has `half_extent = 0` → division by zero on first draw). `pending_click` moved from `Camera` to `Input` (`input.odin`) where it always belonged; camera procs take `^Camera` explicitly; `camera_zoom` stays `proc "contextless"`. Alongside: constants moved to the files that own their concept (package scope is shared across files, so placement is purely for readers) — `DT` → `physics.odin` next to `G`, trail/marker → `render.odin`, view/pick → `camera.odin`; `DT` and `MAX_SIM_SPEED` are `#config(...)`-overridable (`odin run opengl/sim -define:DT=0.00005`) for experiments without edit-recompile-revert; `CreateWindow` now actually uses `SCR_WIDTH`/`SCR_HEIGHT`.

- **Mass mutation** (milestone 17, second half) is done — milestone 17 complete: `,`/`.` halve/double the tracked body's mass (keys chosen for identical physical position on US and Swedish layouts). `pending_mass` in `Input` is a *press count*: the callback adds ±1 and the merged `apply_pending_edits` (`physics.odin`, replaces `apply_pending_vel`; shares the guard/consume skeleton with burns) computes `pow(MASS_FACTOR, count)`, `MASS_FACTOR :: 2` in `input.odin` (interaction knob, not physics). The count is additive — reset/identity 0 — even though its *effect* is multiplicative; an accumulator's reset value is determined by the operation that consumes it. This took three bugs to learn: (1) the first version blended the product and count representations (callback `*= 2`/`/= 2` feeding a `pow` consumer) — up-presses gave ×4 and, via integer division `1/2 = 0`, mass-down was a permanent no-op; (2) the fix guarded the edit blocks with `> 0`, silently dropping the entire negative direction *including retrograde burns* — a regression of a working feature; guards must be `!= 0` (only zero means idle); (3) the early-return kept comparing against the old identity (`== 1`). Lesson: when a value's representation changes, every read/write/guard/reset site must migrate together — grep the field name, check each hit. `compute_accels` runs only in the mass block: stored `Body.accel` holds `G·m/d²` terms computed with the old mass, so the next half-kick would run stale physics (same reason `create_system` ends with the priming call); burns skip it (accel never depends on vel). Observables: a body's own mass cancels in its own acceleration, so mass edits move everyone *else's* orbit; press count is log₂ of the total factor (Moon→Earth-equal ≈ 81× ≈ 6–7 presses, Jupiter→solar mass ≈ 10); one `,` on the Sun puts every near-circular orbiter at exactly escape velocity — `v_circ` for M equals `v_esc` for M/2 — dissolving the system parabolically. Feedback prints use `%f`, which shows the Moon's mass as 0.000000 — `%e` is the honest format for solar masses (known nit).

- **Body spawning** (milestone 18) core is done: right-button press anchors a drag, release spawns the body *at the cursor* with the drag velocity — drag-along semantics, carry-and-let-go (it originally spawned at the press point; the switch is one line in `apply_pending_spawn`, `pos = world_pos_end`, because both endpoints ride in the request). Left button keeps click-to-track untouched — a new feature's guards must not touch a working feature's path. The gesture is stateful, so `Input` holds two Maybes: `drag_start` (set on press, raw pixels) and `pending_spawn: Maybe(Spawn_Request)` (built on release from both endpoints plus mass/radius payload). Raw pixels, not world coords, cross the callback boundary — the world meaning of a pixel depends on camera/window/alpha, all resolved at consume time, so both endpoints are interpreted by the *same* camera even if it tracked a moving body mid-drag. A Maybe's reset is `nil`, not `0` — `0` implicitly converts to a *set* `[2]f64{0,0}`, a legitimate top-left pixel (the reset value is determined by the consumer, which asks `.?`). `calc_world_pos` (`render.odin`) is the exact inverse of `calc_screen_pos` — undo pixel→clip (including the y-flip; screen y grows downward), undo the aspect factor (`* w/h` against the forward's `* h/w`), then `ndc * half_extent + center`; both use *window* coords (not framebuffer — retina), consistent with picking. `apply_pending_spawn` (`physics.odin`) takes `^[dynamic]Body`/`^[dynamic]Trail` — `append` may reallocate, so slices can't do it (main's `bodies[:]` slices are safe because they're re-taken every use) — and is not gated on `tracked_body` (free-cam spawning is the point). The spawned body sets `prev_pos = pos` (else the first-frame render lerp streaks from the origin and the first trail point is garbage) and the proc ends with `compute_accels` — third instance of the priming rule: stored accels must include the newcomer before the next half-kick. Its trail is world-frame (`parent = -1`), `cap = TRAIL_CAP`, `stride = TRAIL_STRIDE_DEFAULT :: 5` (≈ Earth's; the zero value would crash — `record_trail` does `%= stride`). Velocity mapping: `vel = (world_end − world_start) / DRAG_TIME`, `DRAG_TIME :: 0.4` in `input.odin` (interaction knob; originally named `TAU`, renamed because it read as the circle constant 2π) — the drag arrow means "distance traveled in `DRAG_TIME` time units", throw semantics. Because pixel→world divides by `half_extent`, the same pixel drag gives proportionally smaller velocities zoomed in — moon-scale speeds in moon systems for free, the velocity analog of fractional burns. The zero-velocity drop test doubles as a physics demo: a body from rest is a degenerate radial orbit (e=1) that should oscillate through the Sun, but it comes out *hot* — at perihelion it covers ~half a solar radius per step, so the whip is 2–3 wildly-wrong straight-line steps; classic fixed-DT close-encounter failure (future fixes: softening `d²+ε²`, adaptive steps, or collisions/mergers). Preview and mass control landed next — see the following two bullets.

- **Drag preview line** (milestone 18, continued): while the right button is down, main's frame loop polls `glfw.GetCursorPos` — no callback needed; a continuously-sampled value is poll-shaped, and the frame loop sees everything callbacks can't — and `draw_drag_preview` (`render.odin`) draws a 2-point `LINE_STRIP` from anchor to live cursor through the trail program and its shared dynamic VBO. Endpoints run the full pixel→world→NDC round trip (`calc_world_pos` then `calc_ndc_offset`): the camera algebraically cancels for the on-screen line (a pixel drag is the same NDC segment at any zoom), but the round trip keeps the preview on the exact transforms `apply_pending_spawn` consumes — what you preview is what you get by construction — and yields `end_world` as the return value for the mass marker. The trail fade doubles as an arrowhead: with the `count` uniform set to 1, `gl_VertexID` gives alphas 0 and 1, and anchor-first vertex order puts the opaque tip at the cursor — the spawn site. Two shipped bugs, both type-invisible: `BufferSubData` with `2 * size_of(f32)` uploads one *point* (8 bytes), not two — vertex 1 kept whatever the last trail upload left in the shared VBO, which after a spawn is that body's trail origin, so the line visibly ended at the *previous* gesture ("one click behind"; fix: `size_of(scratch_buffer)`, which can't drift); and framebuffer dims passed where `calc_world_pos` needs window dims (retina 2× — preview and spawn would disagree about the world).

- **Spawn mass control** (milestone 18, continued): during a drag the scroll wheel is stolen from zoom — `scroll_callback` branches on `drag_start`, with `camera_zoom` in the `else` so plain scrolling is untouched (side effect: mid-drag zoom, which would shift the drag's world meaning, is now impossible). `spawn_mass_exp: f64` in `Input` accumulates `yOffset * SPAWN_MASS_SENS` (0.5) — f64 because trackpads deliver fractional, high-frequency offsets; an int-per-event counter would jump decades of mass per flick. It's a *setting*, not a command: no consume-and-reset, persists across spawns, identity 0 = Moon mass. That persistence already bit once: banked scrolling from an earlier test left it deeply negative and every spawn came out silently sub-pixel — diagnosed only by putting the number on screen (accepted for now; the title readout is the permanent feedback). `spawn_mass_radius` (`input.odin`) is the single formula site shared by the release callback and the preview: `mass = SPAWN_BASE_MASS · 2^exp`, radius constant-density `r = base · (m/base)^(1/3)` — written `1.0/3.0`, because `1/3` is integer 0 and `pow(x, 0) = 1` silently spawns Moon radius forever (the quiet cousin of milestone 17's `1/2 = 0`). The proc is `proc "contextless"`: callbacks are `proc "c"` with no `context` in scope, and a pure-math leaf shouldn't demand one (the `camera_zoom` precedent). The marker reuses the real body pipeline: `draw_circle` extracted from `draw_bodies`' loop as a *primitive* proc (assumes program/VAO already bound, sets only per-draw uniforms), with batch procs (`draw_bodies`, and `draw_mass_preview` — a batch of one) owning `UseProgram`/`BindVertexArray`; that two-tier split keeps redundant binds out of loops and every `gl.*` call inside `render.odin`. Drawing the future body through the real path means clamp, palette (`palette.Spawn`, white in all three sets), and retina handling are inherited by construction. Two more type-invisible bugs en route, each alone sufficient to hide the marker: `end_ndc` fed where world coords were expected (double transform — pixels, world, and NDC are all `[2]f64`, so the compiler can't catch a space mismatch; proc names and return values are the only guardrail, `distinct` types the heavier cure — applied at milestone's end, see the distinct-coordinate-types bullet), and an unset `color` — zero-value black on a black background. Perception note: the marker clamp hides size feedback until `radius > half_extent × MIN_MARKER_PX / (fb_height/2)` (≈ `half_extent`/150 on retina — at a 1 AU view even the *Sun* clamps), so size only reads when zoomed well in; the title number is the zoom-independent channel.

- **Drag title readout + spawn naming** (milestone 18 complete): while dragging, the title shows the pending spawn's mass (`%e` solar masses plus `2^exp` as a Moon multiple) and drag speed — `|world_end − world_start| / DRAG_TIME × 29.78` km/s, since 1 velocity unit in G=1/AU/M☉ *is* Earth's orbital speed. The drag branch runs *before* the title cache's early-return, rebuilds every frame, and poisons the cache (`prev_sim_speed = -1`) on the way out — tracked body and sim speed don't change during a drag, so without the poison the cache would both suppress per-frame updates and leave stale drag text stuck after release. The first version measured speed in *pixels* (it fetched the window size for `calc_world_pos`, then never called it) — the third space-mixing bug of the milestone and the trigger for the distinct-types migration below; the second version computed the Moon multiple as `2^mass` instead of `2^exp` (≈1.0 forever — plausible-looking, wrong). Spawned bodies are named `Spawnius N` from `State.spawned_bodies`, a counter that only ever increments (once delete exists, dead bodies' numbers must not be reissued). Naming hit both documented traps in one line: `tprintf` (the name dies at the per-frame `free_all(temp_allocator)` and its memory is recycled by later temp allocations — including the title's own `ctprintf`, so the tracked name renders as shifting garbage; `aprintf` heap strings are the fix, to be freed when delete arrives) and `SPAWNED_BODIES :: 0` (a `::` binding is a compile-time constant, so `+ 1` folds to the literal 1 and every body is "1"; runtime counters need `:=` or a struct field — green build ≠ right behavior, lifetimes and mutability aren't in the type system). Alongside: `SECONDS_IN_DAY :: 86400`, and the title strings now use it and `SECONDS_IN_YEAR` instead of inline magic numbers.

- **Distinct coordinate types** (with milestone 18): `Pixel_Pos` and `World_Pos`, both `distinct [2]f64`, declared in `render.odin` above the transform procs that define them. Three space-mixing bugs in one milestone (NDC-as-world in the marker, fb-vs-window dims, pixel-vs-world drag speed) all compiled silently because every space shared one type — so the spaces got types. Scope is deliberately narrow: the transform signatures (`calc_world_pos: Pixel_Pos → World_Pos`, `calc_screen_pos: World_Pos → Pixel_Pos`, `calc_ndc_offset: World_Pos → [2]f64`) and the callback-boundary fields (`pending_click`, `drag_start`, `Spawn_Request` endpoints) — NDC stays raw (short-lived render locals), and `Body`/`Trail`/`Camera` stay `[2]f64` with explicit casts marking the deferred frontier (grep `World_Pos(` and `([2]f64)` to find it). Idioms learned: transforms unwrap-at-entry/wrap-at-exit — the transform body is the one legitimately bilingual zone; distinct array types keep `+`/`-`/swizzles but refuse cross-space arithmetic (exactly the point — the pixel-speed bug is now a compile error); compound literals infer the parameter's type, so `calc_world_pos({end_x, end_y}, …)` needs no cast; and Odin's conversion syntax needs parens around prefix-typed types — `[2]f64(pos)` parses as `[2](f64(pos))` and attempts a scalar cast, so it's `([2]f64)(pos)` or `cast([2]f64)pos`, while identifier types cast plainly as `World_Pos(x)`. Migration method: retype the leaf signatures first and let the compiler enumerate every caller — the automated version of milestone 17's "grep the field name, check each hit" (it found the picking path in `camera.odin` unprompted). 3D will re-ask all of this with `[3]f64`; the pattern is ready.

- **Nested spec tree** (milestone 19 prep): `Body_Spec.parent: int` is gone — specs carry `satellites: []Body_Spec` (legal recursion: a slice is pointer+length, so a struct can hold a slice of itself), and the table is one root literal whose satellites are the planets; the field name dissolves the "planets aren't moons of the Sun" objection — astronomically they *are* satellites. `create_system` delegates to a recursive `add_body(spec, parent_index, ^bodies, ^trails)`: compose from the parent, append, capture `len(bodies) - 1`, recurse into `spec.satellites` with that index. A child literally cannot be processed before its parent — the parent-before-child rule was never an explicit assert, it was the `bodies[spec.parent]` bounds check panicking on wrong order; now the invalid state is unrepresentable. Adding Titan = one literal inside Saturn, zero index counting — the milestone 15 index-shift hazard is dead. Output order is the same DFS as the old flat table, so acceptance was behavioral identity with the pre-refactor build. Bugs on the way: a `bodies[parent_index + 1]` guard-dodge for the root's `-1` read the *wrong* body everywhere and was out of bounds on the very first call (empty array) — the fix was seeing that the existing `if parent_index >= 0` branch already *is* the guard once the parent read moves inside it; the parent is read as a value copy, never a `^Body` (child appends may reallocate — the `apply_pending_spawn` rule again), and a stack copy needs no `delete` — nothing was allocated. The wrapper's `i - 1` parent for top-level specs became a literal `-1`: it happened to compute -1 for one root, but a second root would have silently become a satellite of the first with garbage orbital parameters. Scope note: this fixes *authoring* only — bodies/trails are still index-paired at runtime and `Trail.parent`/`tracked_body` are still indices, which is deletion's problem, not the tree's. Alongside: `Body.spawned: bool` landed as delete groundwork (spawned bodies' `aprintf` names must be freed on delete; spec names are literals that must not be — the flag is the discriminator).

- **Body deletion** (milestone 19) is done: backspace (or forward-delete) removes the tracked body. The request is the established pattern — `pending_delete: bool` in `Input`, set in the key callback, consumed by `apply_pending_delete` (`physics.odin`) once per frame outside the drain loop, *before* `apply_pending_edits` so a same-frame burn/mass press aimed at the just-deleted body dies on the `tracked_body < 0` guard instead of editing whoever slid into the slot. Delete is gated on `PRESS` only, not `PRESS|REPEAT` like the other keys — holding the key would fire per-repeat requests that are harmless only by coincidence (the first delete drops tracking to -1 and the guard eats the rest); a bool's consume-and-reset identity is `false`. Internal order is load-bearing: free the name *before* `ordered_remove` (afterwards it's unreachable — a silent leak, not a crash), and only when `Body.spawned` — `aprintf` heap names must be freed, spec-literal names must never be. Both `ordered_remove`s sit on adjacent lines: bodies and trails are index-paired, and deleting from one array is the pairing invariant's one dangerous moment. Then the fixup over the surviving trails, two provably-exclusive branches: `parent == deleted` → orphan (parent = -1, ring cleared, cap/stride/frame_count reset to the spawn-trail shape — the recorded points are *parent-relative*, so re-anchoring them to the world without clearing draws garbage streaks through the origin); `parent > deleted` → decrement. The shipped bug was the missing second branch: deleting the Moon left the Galilean trails at `parent = 6`, which after the shift is *Io* — Io's trail anchored to itself and its siblings' Jupiter-relative arcs swam around Io. Lesson: an index fixup has *two* cases, the hole and everything above it; handling only the hole looks correct in every test that doesn't have parented bodies past the deleted index. `tracked_body` goes to -1 (delete only targets the tracked body, so no decrement case) — which is also why the title cache needs no poison: the tracked index always changes value, so the cache comparison misses naturally. The proc ends with `compute_accels` — fourth instance, and the priming rule's wording is now "any operation that changes masses, adds, *or removes* bodies": survivors' stored accels still contain the dead body's pull, and the next half-kick would apply ghost gravity. Observables that confirmed it: Galilean trails stay glued to Jupiter when the Moon dies; Earth's death puts the Moon on a solar orbit with a clean restarted trail; spawn → delete → spawn yields `Spawnius 2` (numbers never reissued, freed name never touched); deleting the Sun sends everything off in straight-ish lines *and the scene drifts* — momentum zeroing was a statement about the initial system, not a conservation law the sim enforces. Accepted wart: the Sun's wobble trail keeps its Jupiter-era cap/stride if Jupiter is deleted.

- **Code-review hardening** (between milestones 19 and 20): a full-package review (Odin idioms, naming, structure, OpenGL conventions) turned into ~26 small commits; the package now passes `odin check -vet -strict-style` clean, and that's the standing lint bar. Structure: the trail concept got its own `trail.odin` (`Trail`, `trail_make_orbital`/`trail_make_default`/`trail_record`) — delete's six-field hand reset collapsed into `trail_make_default()`, so the `frame_count = stride − 1` anchor trick lives in exactly one place; the `pending_*_apply` procs moved from `physics.odin` to `interaction.odin` (they're the consume side of the input pattern, not physics — `physics.odin` is the integrator again); `Body` moved to `body.odin`; the GLFW callbacks moved from `main.odin` to `input.odin`. Naming settled on noun-first (`trail_record`, `bodies_draw`, `circle_draw`, `state_get`, `callback_*`) — in a flat single package the prefix is the namespace, so related procs sort together — and the camelCase stragglers (`bodyA`, `posX`, `yOffset`, `VBO` locals) went snake_case. The `@(static)` title-cache locals — package-level mutable globals in a trench coat, quietly violating the State rule — became a `title_stale: bool` in `State`, set at every mutation site (speed keys, track/untrack, delete, and per-frame during a drag so the normal title rebuilds after release); side win: speed changes now refresh the title while tracking. The palettes became `::` constants over an enum-indexed `[Body_Name]Color` (a new enum value breaks every incomplete palette at compile time), `PALETTE :: REALISTIC.body`, and `specs` went `@(rodata)` — unlocked by replacing the two `math.pow10` calls with e-notation literals, since read-only data needs constant initializers; the package now has zero mutable globals, no exceptions. OpenGL: uniform locations are looked up once at load into `Body_Program`/`Trail_Program` structs (macOS never got GL 4.3's explicit uniform locations, so caching is the way), which also deleted a footgun — the old `shader_set_*(program_id, name, …)` signature looked program-agnostic but `glUniform*` writes to whatever's *bound*; `gl_check_error` drains `gl.GetError` once per frame under `when ODIN_DEBUG` (KHR_debug is 4.3+ too); `ClearColor` moved to init (persistent GL state); shader-load failures now say which pair died. Guardrail: `create_system` asserts exactly one root — the `trails[0]` cap fix-up and largest-mass scan silently assumed it, and a second root's cap-0 trail would have crashed `trail_record`'s `% cap` on the first physics step (found by review, not by a crash). Two Odin-shaped lessons: `os.exit` skips defers, so `defer glfw.Terminate()` only covers the normal exit path and error paths must clean up explicitly; and a `when ODIN_DEBUG` block compiles out entirely, taking an import's only use with it — release `-vet` then flags the import as unused, and the idiomatic anchor is a blank binding, `_ :: fmt`. The review's one open major became milestone 20: `accels_compute` still divides by bare `r²`, so spawning onto an existing body NaN-explodes the system — softening and/or merge-on-contact deferred as a proper milestone rather than a drive-by fix.

- **Trail perf at spawn scale** (perf-improvements milestone, first pass): the worry was spawn-spam — hundreds of user-spawned bodies tanking the frame — and the plan was policy first: spawned/orphaned trails don't need Earth-grade budgets, so `trail_make_default` now uses `SPAWN_TRAIL_CAP :: 1280` / `SPAWN_TRAIL_STRIDE :: 25` (both `#config`-overridable; coverage 32,000 steps ≈ half an Earth orbit; the split favors halving coverage over doubling stride because angularity is governed by the *fastest* orbit — a 0.1 AU sun-grazer gets ~80 segments/orbit — not the average one), with orphans sharing the budget deliberately: deleting Jupiter must not mint four worst-case trails. To prove the predicted 10×, `measure.odin` landed behind `MEASURE :: #config(MEASURE, false)` — 300 seeded circular orbits (0.5–5 AU, real spawn conventions: `aprintf` names, `spawned = true`, `prev_pos = pos`, priming `accels_compute`), per-second `physics`/`trails_draw` timers plus a ring-fill readout, and before/after runs via `-define:SPAWN_TRAIL_CAP=12800 -define:SPAWN_TRAIL_STRIDE=5` with zero git churn. The measurement falsified the prediction in its first line: `trails_draw` was ~59 ms with rings *nearly empty* (15 points recorded) and only crept to ~65 ms at 2.4M points/frame — the cost was ~185 µs per *trail*, flat in points (linearity confirmed with `-define:MEASURE_SPAWN_COUNT=50`: 65 trails ≈ 11.8 ms, the same per-trail figure), so the per-point term the cap cut attacked was ~6 ms of the 59. Diagnosis: every trail uploads into the *one shared* trail VBO via `BufferSubData` while the previous trail's `DrawArrays` may still be reading it — an implicit GPU sync per trail, the classic streaming-buffer stall, invisible at 15 trails and fatal at 315. The fix is the canonical orphaning idiom: `gl.BufferData(…, nil, gl.STREAM_DRAW)` immediately before each upload (the driver hands over fresh backing storage; pending draws keep reading the old one; usage hint switched from `DYNAMIC_DRAW` to match) — applied in `trails_draw` and in `drag_preview_draw`, which writes the same VBO once per drag frame and is the same hazard in miniature. Result: ~59 ms → ~1.6 ms at 315 trails (~35× — one line), back under vsync at 120 Hz. Rule recorded: *buffers written every frame orphan before upload; buffers written once (circle mesh, `STATIC_DRAW`) never do*. One bug en route, another value-where-type-expected trap: `size_of(32)` compiles and returns 8 — the size of the untyped-int literal's default type — silently doubling the orphaned allocation versus the creation size; `size_of(f32)` was meant (the quiet cousin of `1/3 = 0` and `[2]f64(v)`). Lessons: measure before optimizing — a cost that ignores the term being scaled is fixed overhead, and the very first report line (near-empty rings, full price) already said so; and a CPU timer bracketing GL submission is exactly the right instrument for sync stalls, which bill the submitting thread, while GPU-side cost hides in `SwapBuffers`. What the fix deliberately did not touch, on schedule: physics — at high sim speed with 315 bodies the drain loop plateaus at ~154 ms/frame, the 0.1 s `frame_time` clamp doing its spiral-of-death job (sim time falls behind the knob instead of the frame rate collapsing without bound — though ~7 fps is no prize). 315 bodies is ~49,500 force pairs per step versus 105 at fifteen; the deferred GL stages (persistent per-trail buffers, shader-side ring-age fade, body instancing) are now optional refinements, and the perf frontier moved to the integrator — frame-budgeted draining, SoA layout, Barnes–Hut — a future milestone. The harness stays in-tree as the standing benchmark (inert without the define). Open nits: `TRAIL_STRIDE_DEFAULT` is dead but still declared, and the new `#config` knobs have no `#assert` guards (a cap above `TRAIL_CAP` is a runtime bounds panic waiting in `trail_record`).

- **Frame-budgeted drain loop** (milestone 21) is done: physics gets a fixed wall-clock slice of each frame — `PHYSICS_BUDGET :: #config(PHYSICS_BUDGET, 0.005)` seconds — and the frame rate never pays for overload again. The old loop had an inflow cap but no outflow cap: the 0.1 s `frame_time` clamp bounded the debt a frame could *add*, but the drain ran every accumulated step no matter the cost, equilibrating at 315 bodies at ~154 ms/frame — 7 fps, ~97% of wall time in physics, and sim time *still* falling behind the knob (0.1 s of admitted debt per 0.154 s frame ≈ 65% of what the clamp let in). The fix is a `deadline` read just before the loop and a break checked *after* each step — after, not before, is the progress guarantee: a budget smaller than one step's cost degrades to one-step-per-frame, never to frozen — then the leftover debt is dropped to `accumulator mod DT` right after the loop. That drop is the alpha invariant's bodyguard: `alpha := accumulator / DT` silently assumes the loop only exits below DT, and a budget break holding 40 DT of undrained debt makes alpha 40 — `prev_pos + (pos − prev_pos) × 40` renders every body 39 steps *ahead* of computed physics, extrapolate-and-snap (the accumulator's meaning changed at the break; alpha was the read site that had to migrate with it — milestone 17's lesson in new clothes). `mod` rather than `= 0` keeps the fractional remainder so alpha stays continuous under marginal overload; interpolation only matters visually at low speed and the budget only binds at high load, so the regimes never overlap. The default is written `0.005` (seconds — `glfw.GetTime`'s unit): a `5`-milliseconds default with a `/ 1000` somewhere would be the `1/3 = 0` trap making the budget silently zero, which the progress guarantee would then disguise as one-step-per-frame forever. Measurement (a steps column in the harness, reset per interval like its neighbors): ~120 µs/step at 315 bodies → ~5,000 steps/sec inside the 5 ms budget ≈ 29 days/sec — half a percent of the old 15 yr/sec ceiling, which alone demands ~940k steps/sec against the machine's ~8k at full wall. No drain policy closes a 100× O(n²) gap (steps/sec tracked pair count almost exactly across 50/100/300 spawns: 160k/45k/5k against pair ratios 1:3.2:24); the budget traded peak throughput (97% → 60% of wall) for interactivity, and the throughput frontier itself belongs to DT and Barnes–Hut. That measurement killed the planned effective-speed title readout in favor of a *governor*: when overload persists `GOVERNOR_FRAMES` (30 ≈ 0.25 s) consecutive frames, the sim halves `sim_speed` itself — the same `math.max(1, sim_speed / 2)` expression as the ← key, so the machine's writes stay on the user's halve/double ladder and share the floor (int halving reaches 0 = zero inflow = overload can never fire again, a permanently dead knob), plus `title_stale` because the governor is a mutation site like any speed key. Halving is its own hysteresis — each step overshoots downward, creating margin, so no hunting — and there is deliberately no auto-raise: recovery is the → key, keeping `sim_speed` single-writer-per-direction. Division of labor: the drop is *mechanism* — per-frame, silent, absorbs spikes (a title-bar-drag stall dumps 0.1 s of clamped debt; the drop forgives it instantly and the consecutive counter reads it as one overloaded frame and resets); the governor is *policy* — sub-second, visible, moves the knob honestly, so the existing title readout tells the truth with zero new display machinery. `overload_frames` lives as a `main` local beside `accumulator`, not in `State` — callbacks never touch it, and `State` exists for callback access. The shipped bug: the governor went in as a declared counter plus threshold check with *no increment and no reset* — dead code that compiles clean; a counter is three sites (write, reset, read) and all three must exist, the quieter cousin of consume-and-reset. And the reset-on-clean-frame else-branch is load-bearing: without it the counter accumulates scattered overloaded frames across minutes and eventually halves on noise. `MAX_SIM_SPEED` rose to 50 yr/sec in the same pass — the knob is now a *request* the governor reconciles with reality, so the static ceiling no longer has to be conservative.

- **Merge-on-contact** (milestone 22) is done: overlapping bodies (`d < r_a + r_b`, real radii) become one, in `collision.odin`; removal reuses `body_remove`, extracted from delete. The surprise that framed it: spawn-on-Sun never NaN'd (exact `d = 0` is measure-zero) — the slingshots were *under-resolved integration*, 2–3 wrong straight-line steps through the deep potential injecting energy. Softening stayed on the shelf: a moon-safe ε can't reach the distances where Sun encounters go wrong, while contact with big bodies is detected reliably (a Sun-diver moves ≤ ~0.45 solar radii per step).
  - `vel` and `pos` are the same formula twice — the mass-weighted average is the pair's center of mass and its momentum over total mass — so the merge is invisible to the rest of the system. `prev_pos` gets the same weighting (the teleport rule's principled form). The energy and pair-spin that vanish are heat, not bugs.
  - Shipped bug: `survivor` is a pointer into the array, and writing `survivor.mass` before the vel formula read `body_a.mass` injected momentum. Procs that read and mutate through aliases snapshot into value copies first — milestone 19's value-copy lesson, second appearance.
  - Merges run at the *top of each drain iteration*, merge-until-clean (loop-and-a-half), so no step ever executes on an overlapping pair. One-merge-per-iteration left the rest of a pile-up stepping on bare `1/d²` — bodies escaped between checks *with merging on*; a post-drain sweep can't help because the kicks happen mid-drain.
  - `tracked_body` needed the fixup delete never did (delete removes only the tracked body; merge removes arbitrary indices): retarget-then-decrement as two sequential `if`s — "the hole and everything above it," third field.
  - Detection priced at ~158 vs ~120 µs/step at 315 bodies (+~30%, a second O(n²) pass; squared-distance compare is the known cheapening).
  - A heavy spawn in the 300-body measurement disk ran away — cold circular orbits → huge gravitational focusing → each merge feeds the next; 315 bodies → single digits in ~1.5 sim-years. Real accretion physics, but diagnosed backwards from microseconds: nothing printed merges or body count — the put-the-number-on-screen lesson again.
  - Accepted residuals: small-body pairs tunnel (~8.6 radii/step at 1 velocity unit) and near-misses still whip — swept detection, softening, or smaller DT, if they ever annoy.

- **Barnes–Hut** (milestone 23) is done: `quadtree.odin` — a pooled quadtree rebuilt every physics step (bounding square → insert → mass/COM summarize) and a traversal that treats any node with `s/d < BH_THETA` (0.5) as a point mass at its COM. `accels_compute` dispatches on body count: exact pairwise below `BH_THRESHOLD` (300, the measured crossover), tree above — the brute loop survives as the small-n fast path and the correctness oracle (`BH_VALIDATE` re-derives per-body brute accels in-step and tracks max relative error; `BH_DEBUG` prints per-second tree invariants).
  - Pool discipline cuts both ways: `node := tree.node[i]` is a value copy, and the first build wrote every mutation into copies — nothing persisted, and the insert recursion re-processed the same forever-empty node to stack overflow. Mutations are indexed writes (`tree.node[i].field = …`), and nothing taken before an `append` survives it. But traversal and summarize never append — copies and `&node` iteration are safe and right there. The rule was always "no stale references across an append," not "never copy."
  - A just-split leaf has no children yet, so re-inserting its old occupant through the normal insert found "empty leaf" and re-claimed the slot — infinite claim/split at constant depth, under the depth assert's radar (depth never grows along the cycle). Fix: `quadtree_push_down` always descends (create the child, recurse into it); both the internal case and the split case route through it. Discriminant: any child → internal; no children + no body → claimable; body → split.
  - Children are appended after their parent, so child index > parent index always — `#reverse for` over the pool *is* a bottom-up traversal; summarize needs no recursion. Its leaf branch needs `continue`: fall-through into the (empty) children-sum zeroed every leaf's mass and poisoned every total above.
  - Zero-value `children` is `{0, 0, 0, 0}` — four valid indices aimed at the root. The `-1` sentinels exist only because the single `create_node` site writes them; node creation stays one proc for exactly that reason.
  - A false `when` block parses but is never type-checked: the validate block shipped broken while the default check stayed green. Every define combination is its own compile — lint the matrix, not just the default. (Paid three more times as stranded imports: `fmt`, `glfw`, `math`.)
  - θ=0 is a *structural* oracle — the MAC never accepts, so the tree sums exactly the brute-force terms (measured max relative error ~7e-16) — but it never reads `mass`, `com`, or `s/d`, so a wrong-COM bug would sail through; the θ=0.5 run (~3.2e-4 baseline, ~1e-2 spikes when a body catches a node at s/d just under θ) is the test of the aggregates.
  - No softening needed, structurally: merge-until-clean keeps coincident pairs out of the build (bounded subdivision depth), the containment guard plus COM-inside-the-box (convexity) keeps aggregate d > 0, and self-interaction dies on an index compare, not a distance check.
  - Measured (µs/step, `-o:speed`): 115 bodies 16 brute / 31 tree; 315: 144/137; 1015: 1540/810; 3015: 15400/6440 — crossover ≈ 300, now the shipped threshold. The 3015 ratio is only 2.4× because the O(n²) collision sweep is a shared floor (~half the tree column; collision-disabled runs confirmed gravity-only ~4×): Barnes–Hut did its job and the bottleneck moved — quadtree-served collision detection is the named successor milestone.

- **Quadtree collision detection** (milestone 24) is done: `collision_compute` dispatches on the same `BH_THRESHOLD` as gravity — the pair scan below (now `collision_scan`, the oracle), a per-body tree query above. `quadtree_contact` mirrors `quadtree_accel`'s shape but prunes with a point-to-box distance test against the node's box expanded by `body.radius + r_max` (global max radius, O(n) scan per call); leaves run the one shared `bodies_overlap` test — extracted from the pair scan and switched to squared-distance compare, retiring milestone 22's "known cheapening" note. First-hit-wins preserves `collision_compute`'s exact contract, so main's merge-until-clean loop is untouched.
  - The index-staleness trap never materialized — the priming rule already covers it: `body_remove` ends with `accels_compute`, which on the tree path rebuilds the tree with fresh positions *and* indices after every merge, including mid-cascade. Verified by reading before building; invariants compound, and the four-milestone-old rule paid out here for free.
  - One threshold, deliberately shared: not because the crossovers coincide (brute collision is cheaper per pair, so its true crossover is higher) but because the tree only *exists* when gravity's branch builds it — a separate collision threshold would create states that query a stale or never-built tree.
  - The `max(0, …)` overhang clamp in `box_within_reach` is load-bearing: a body inside the box on one axis otherwise contributes a negative overhang whose square inflates the distance — silent misses. Same failure class as forgetting the querying body's own radius in the reach; both present as milestone-22-style tunneling, not crashes.
  - No-miss is the only correct oracle property: with simultaneous overlaps, tree and brute may legally return *different* pairs (merge-until-clean makes the difference immaterial), so the `BH_VALIDATE` assert is "brute found ⇒ tree found," nothing stronger. ~12k steps validated clean at 315 bodies, threshold 0.
  - Measured (tree path, `-o:speed`): collision 0.4 ms → 72 µs/step at 1015 bodies; ~3.5 ms → 0.38 ms at 3015. Total step at 3015: 6.4 → 3.2 ms — 2× on top of Barnes–Hut, ~4.9× vs full brute. At 3000 bodies the frame is now `trails_draw`-bound (~17.6 ms, ~40 fps): the bottleneck has left physics entirely, and the next perf frontier is the milestone-20 deferred render work (per-trail fixed cost — persistent buffers, instancing).

- **3D** (milestone 25) is done: three packages — a dimension-generic `sim_core` (`Vec :: [DIM]f64`, `#config(DIM, 2)`) under two thin apps — with the 3D app at feature parity minus spawn: orbit camera, billboarded bodies, fading trails, screen-space picking, tracking, edits, delete, title. The phase-by-phase record lives in PLAN_3D.md; the lessons:
  - `Vec` is an alias, so the DIM=2 build proves nothing about genericization completeness — leftover `[2]f64` annotations and `{a, b}` element-count literals only surface at the DIM=3 type-check. The compiler is the auditor, but only in the dimension you ask it to check; the define matrix gained a DIM axis and a script (`scripts/check.sh`, one cell per app × define combination).
  - The planar-embedding oracle passed bitwise on both gravity paths: from the planar spec start every z-term is `0·x` and every distance gains `+0.0` — both exact in IEEE — so 3D x/y matched the 2D binary bit-for-bit over 20k steps, forced-tree path included (the z-octant bit is always 0, so the octree *is* the quadtree with the same child indices). Positions-as-bit-patterns (`DETERMINISM_STEPS`) is now the standing cross-build oracle.
  - `BH_THRESHOLD` re-measured in 3D: crossover ~600 vs 2D's 300 (8-way branching and wider vectors raise the tree's constant), insensitive to disk thickness; shipped as a DIM-aware default. Measured against a z-jittered spawn disk — a planar disk never exercises the z-split and would have benchmarked the degenerate tree.
  - The trail ring became a heap slice sized to its logical cap (the inline `[TRAIL_CAP]Vec` hit ~307 KB at DIM=3 — Odin's large-stack warning). Ownership inverted the value-copy lesson: a `Trail` is now a *handle* — slice assignment shares the ring, only `make` mints independence — and the root-trail cap fix-up shipped exactly that aliasing bug (Sun and Jupiter recording into one array). Rings are freed in `body_remove` *before* `ordered_remove` (after, they're unreachable), and `Trail{}`'s nil ring upgrades "never hand-build a Trail" from convention to memory safety. Spawn-trail memory fell ~10× as a side effect.
  - `when` blocks don't open a scope (inner `:=` collides with the enclosing one), and moving code *out* of a `when` re-exposes it to every build: the headless runner, extracted to a proc, suddenly type-checked `0 ..< TOTAL_STEPS` — a constant-empty interval, which Odin rejects — in windowed builds. Whole-file `when` gates with `_ :: pkg` import anchors are the pattern (`headless.odin`, `measure.odin`); `ols.json` checker profiles teach the editor the same define matrix.
  - The 3D render pipeline inverts 2D's division of labor: the CPU no longer produces NDC — it produces camera-relative f64 positions and one `mvp` uniform does the rest on the GPU. The view matrix is built with the eye at the origin, so a forgotten `pos − eye` is loud (garbage on screen), not subtle (f32 jitter at moon zoom). Billboarding cost nothing: model-view = view-space translate·scale with no rotation, so the 2D circle mesh faces the camera by construction.
  - GL's silent-failure catalog grew three entries: `BlendFunc` without `Enable(BLEND)` ignores alpha entirely (the trail fade "vanished"); `DepthMask(false)` left set makes the next depth *clear* a no-op (ghosting that presents as an unrelated bug); NaN geometry rasterizes nothing and raises no GL error — a zero-value `Camera.distance` divided into the projection, the `half_extent = 0` lesson's 3D edition. And `gl_check_error` only exists under `-debug`, which plain `odin run` doesn't set — the errors it would have caught stayed invisible.
  - Two old lessons re-fired in new clothes: `alpha` computed *before* the drain loop is undrained step debt, not a leftover fraction (milestone 21's invariant, second act — everything rendered a few steps extrapolated, subtly); and a proc-local `prev_cursor` is reborn zero every call, so the orbit drag applied absolute cursor positions as deltas — frame-persistent state lives in `main` locals beside `accumulator`, passed by pointer.
  - Picking stayed screen-space: project bodies through the forward transforms (`clip.w` *is* view depth; skip `w <= 0` before dividing) and hit-test in window pixels against the clamped marker — the 2D "sub-pixel bodies are clickable" lesson transfers whole. Rays wait until a click must produce a world *position* (spawn). One button serves click and orbit-drag, disambiguated by release displacement.
  - The "3D runs at half speed" investigation ended with nothing wrong: the third component costs 1.42×/step (207 → 295 ns, measured as a slope between two step counts — a single timing point silently includes process startup), which pushed the 50 yr/sec drain across the 5 ms `PHYSICS_BUDGET` (2D 5.4 ms — barely holds; 3D 7.7 ms — can't), and the governor's power-of-2 ladder rounded the shortfall to one visible rung. Three systems each doing its job composed into a misleading symptom; 3D's budget default is now 0.010, a deliberate divergence trading vsync headroom for the higher rung.

- **Lighting** (milestone 27) is done: Lambert diffuse with an ambient floor, computed on sphere-impostor normals over the existing billboards — no distance attenuation (real 1/d² would light Neptune at 0.1% of Earth) and no specular. The light source is a per-frame most-massive-body scan gated by `MIN_STAR_MASS :: 0.08` (the hydrogen-burning limit): the star draws emissive; no star above threshold → ambient only. Shading multiplies in linear space (gamma 2.2 sandwich) and `PALETTE` switched to `ALBEDO` — the palette lighting was authored for. Entirely in the 3D app's render layer; `sim_core` untouched. Built in five stages each with its own observable, starting with an `mvp` → `mv` + `proj` split as a pixel-identical refactor (lighting needs view-space positions in the fragment shader; a premultiplied `mvp` destroys them).
  - Impostor normals: lighting consumes *normals*, not geometry. `N = vec3(local, sqrt(max(0.0, 1.0 - r2)))` is the exact sphere normal — exact because the billboard model-view has no rotation, so the disc's local axes *are* view axes and no normal matrix exists anywhere. The `max(0, …)` is mandatory: rim vertices sit at r² = 1 and f32 interpolation lands slightly above → NaN rim.
  - The emissive branch must *jump over* the lighting math, not override its result: at the sun's own fragments `normalize(sun_pos − frag_pos)` is normalize(~0) → NaN, and NaN fragments vanish with no GL error.
  - Zero-value trap, GL edition: `Body_Program` grew `emissive`/`lit` locations, the return literal omitted them, and the fields zero-initialized to location **0** — a *valid* location owned by another uniform. Writes landed somewhere real with no error while the shader's flags stayed at default 0 and the sun rendered invisible — the quadtree's `children = {0,0,0,0}` lesson in GL clothes. The copy-pasted diagnostics (`if mv == -1` after every lookup) were dead, so the warning system built for exactly this never fired.
  - Per-draw uniforms are set *before* the draw they govern: `emissive` was set after `circle_draw`, so body i drew with body i−1's flag and the sun — drawn first — got last frame's leftovers.
  - GLSL shadowing is legal and silent: `float diffuse = …` inside the `if (lit)` block declared a fresh variable, the outer `diffuse` stayed 0, and every lit body multiplied to black.
  - Blending is enabled globally for the trail fade, so every shader's alpha channel is live — harmless while alpha was a literal 1.0, load-bearing the moment lighting math touched the output vector. Related discriminator: on a black background *dark* and *transparent* are indistinguishable — swap `ClearColor` to tell them apart; the "transparent" dim bodies were the trail tip honestly crossing the billboard plane, pre-existing geometry exposed by the new contrast.
  - Gamma: albedo × N·L belongs in linear space — `pow(color, 2.2)` in, `pow(result, 1/2.2)` out, non-emissive branch only (decode∘encode is identity, so the emissive branch needs nothing). The terminator widens, and `AMBIENT`'s meaning changes under the sandwich — tune it after choosing, not before.
  - Observables: Venus runs crescent → gibbous around its orbit (Galileo's argument against geocentrism); deleting the Sun turns the lights out; doubling Jupiter past 0.08 M☉ ignites it. Eclipses are absent *by design* — N·L asks which way a surface faces, never whether something sits between it and the sun; cast shadows are an occlusion term, milestone 28's "shadow".

- **Binary-correct setup** (milestone 31) is done: pair gravitational parameters are `G(M+m)` everywhere (`mu` feeds mean motion, the perifocal velocities, and the trail-stride period alike), the planets' heliocentric element rows became *system barycenters* (Horizons targets 1–9), and `body_add` reflex-splits each parent about its direct satellites — the element math extracted into a pure `spec_rel_state` leaf that returns bare relative state. Verified against Horizons state vectors: epoch state at the transcription floor for all 22 bodies (Charon's relative velocity error 5.6e-2 → 2.6e-4), and 223-day Kepler propagation error for Earth 940,000 km → 33,000 km. `SECONDS_IN_YEAR` became the exact Julian year in the same milestone.
  - Milestone 30 had already half-done this milestone without saying so: body-center osculating rows *encode* the barycentric wobble at the epoch, so placement-at-epoch was never wrong — the real defects were the pair μ and *propagation*. The milestone's roadmap wording predated that understanding; measurement rewrote the scope.
  - The propagation surprise: the predicted error of a body-center conic was the wobble's ~4,700 km amplitude; the measured error was 940,000 km. The Moon's pull pollutes the body-center row's semi-major axis (a = 0.999195 vs the EMB's 1.000002), and a wrong `a` is a wrong *period* through `n ∝ a^-1.5` — a bounded spatial wobble becomes an unbounded clock error. The barycenter row fixes the clock, not just the offset. Mercury–Neptune sat at ~20–60k km before *and* after: that's genuine planet-planet perturbation drift, the honest floor of any fixed conic.
  - Barycenter rows and reflex code are one commit by necessity: either alone double-counts the wobble — the data *is* half the algorithm. Frame discipline's fourth appearance, now with an atomicity rule.
  - The reflex is a two-pass placement with no post-append mutation: children's relative states are computed (pure leaf, arguments only) before the parent's absolute state is finalized, so the parent appends once, already correct — no indexed-write fixup, no `prev_pos` resync. Purity is load-bearing: each satellite's `rel` is computed twice (reflex pass, child's own add) and must be bit-identical. Two different mass sums serve two different jobs: pair μ is `G(spec + sat)`, the reflex divisor is the whole system's — swapping them is a silent ~1e-4.
  - Four composition bugs shipped en route — μ with masses *multiplied* (dimensionally mass², velocities 577× slow; the project's third multiply-where-add-belongs), the grandparent passed into a leaf that composed absolutely, the parent composition dropped entirely (every moon placed at origin-distance), and `parent.pos` added to a *velocity* — and all four were invisible for planets because the Sun sits at the origin with zero velocity at add time. Moons are where composition bugs surface; pos and vel sharing `Vec` is the space-mixing class in time-derivative clothes; and the Horizons epoch diff caught every one of them — the auditor the compiler cannot be.
  - The exact Julian year dropped the velocity floor 5.7e-5 → 1.9e-5 — and the residual is the gap between two *kinds* of year: the calendar's Julian year vs the dynamical year (2π·√(AU³/GM), ~596 s longer). `SECONDS_IN_YEAR` serves two masters — calendar readouts and the physics time unit — and stays calendar-anchored by choice; splitting the anchors is the known 20× if it ever matters.
  - The victory lap became a new instrument: integrating the committed January snapshot forward 223 days, the sim *predicted* the 2026-08-12 solar eclipse — minimum separation 0.890° at 18:13 UTC against reality's ~0.89° at 17:46 — where frozen-element lookup was 10.7 hours and 1.84° wrong. Lunar nodal regression isn't modeled anywhere; it *emerges* from N-body gravity. The 27-minute residual is the initial-condition budget (spec-mass rounding, the year-anchor gap). Lookup degrades at the conic's rate, prediction at the integrator's — that distinction became milestone 32.

- **Launch-time catch-up** (milestone 32) is done: a no-args launch builds the system at the spec epoch and integrates the shared drain trio (`collision_drain` → `physics_step` → `trail_record`) forward to the launch minute, so the start state is the N-body integrator's prediction instead of a frozen-element conic lookup — launching now is bitwise identical to having launched at the epoch and run since, because it *is* the same loop. Forward `START_JD` pins integrate too; pre-epoch pins keep the conic jump (Kepler handles negative Δt, forward integration doesn't). Cost today: 38,305 steps, ~50 ms optimized / ~220 ms debug, growing ~63k steps per elapsed year. (`collisions_drain` → `collision_drain` rename rode along.)
  - The tempting one-liner — seed the epoch-to-now gap into the accumulator and let the frame loop drain it — fails structurally twice: the overload branch drops debt *by design* (`mod(accumulator, DT)` would discard months after one over-budget frame), and the governor would read the debt as overload and halve `sim_speed`. Catch-up must be a dedicated pre-loop; the accumulator receives only the sub-`DT` remainder, too small for either mechanism to see.
  - The date readout needed zero new code: reassign `start_jd = JD_EPOCH`, set `sim_time = n·DT`, and `current_jd = start_jd + sim_time` is honest by the existing arithmetic. The `if catch_up` has no else — the branch choice already happened at `spec_delta_t`, and the `if` is post-processing only the integrate path needs; an else would restate zero-values already true.
  - Deterministic builds needed no gating: they force `start_jd = JD_EPOCH`, so `n = 0` falls out of the arithmetic — pre/post refactor dumps bitwise identical. Composition bonus: a deterministic build with a *pinned* forward `START_JD` now catches up before dumping, so the app itself emits launch-minute states — the scratch-package Horizons harness has an in-tree replacement.
  - Acceptance (Horizons vectors at the exact dump JD, integrated vs conic at the same instant) is a map of *where* perturbations matter: Moon 1,459 km vs 40,594 (28× — ≈24 lunar minutes of phase vs ~11 hours), Saturn 2,927 vs 36,981 (12.6× — Jupiter's pull), Mars 9,285 vs 16,720. Earth ~10,560 km *both ways*: heliocentric Earth at 222 days is transcription-floor-dominated, not perturbation-dominated. Venus/Jupiter/Charon show conic marginally "better" — floor noise, not signal; integrating doesn't move the floors, it stops the divergence above them.

- **Eclipse shadows** (milestone 33) are done: an analytic occlusion term on the existing lighting — per fragment, the fraction of the Sun's disc left visible behind every other body's disc (angular radii plus separation, circle–circle overlap area), multiplied into the Lambert diffuse only; ambient floor and emissive branch untouched. Penumbra, totality, and annularity are the three regimes of one function, and solar and lunar eclipses both fall out of the same occluder loop — nothing in the code knows what an eclipse is. Shadow mapping was never on the table (depth precision dies at AU scale, and it would have demanded real geometry); no sphere meshes were needed either, because the impostor normals already exist and the fragment's sphere-surface point is one reconstruction line, `P = center + N·radius`. Physics-neutrality proven the usual way: 100-step dump bitwise identical pre/post, 13-cell matrix clean.
  - The two radii part ways in `circle_draw`: the *clamped* `draw_radius` goes into `mv` (how big the disc renders), the *physical* radius into the reconstruction uniform (where the shader believes the fragments are). A marker-clamped disc therefore shows shadows at fractionally-correct positions — the marker is a magnified icon of the body and the eclipse rides along. Crossing the two is silent, and wrong exactly when zoomed out.
  - f32 `acos(dot)` of nearly-parallel unit vectors quantizes at ~√ε ≈ 60 arcsec — the Sun's whole disc is ~16 arcmin, so the penumbra would band and shimmer. Small angles come from `atan(|a×b|, a·b)`, robust at every scale; the `acos` calls inside the overlap-area formula are fine because clamps bound their arguments. Anywhere angles get small, `acos` of a dot product is the wrong inverse.
  - "Declared" is not "active": GLSL dead-strips uniforms that don't reach the output, and `GetUniformLocation` returns −1 for them — the six new uniforms warned for two commits until the visibility loop made them matter. Writes to −1 are silent no-ops by spec, making −1 the *safe* sentinel and location 0 the dangerous default — the inverse of what zero-values suggest. The milestone-27 location-0 trap fired again en route (lookups present, return literal incomplete) and this time died structurally: `uniform_lookup` names each uniform exactly once, so no second list exists to forget — and the surviving warning print is a free dead-uniform detector.
  - The shader loop is bounded by `occluder_count`, never the array size: slots past the count hold stale uploads from earlier frames, so a deleted body would keep casting ghost shadows from its last position. The count is what makes the tail meaningless — consume-and-reset, GPU edition.
  - Receiver identity crosses to the GPU as an occluder-array *slot*, with −1 as "not a participant" covering three cases at once: the light source, cap-truncated bodies, starless frames (truncated bodies still receive shadows — they just don't cast). `MAX_OCCLUDERS` exists twice because GLSL has no include; keep-in-sync comments are the only guard. Spec-first body ordering makes first-come truncation accidentally priority-correct — the bodies dropped at the cap are the freshest spawns, the least likely casters.
  - GLSL lives outside the lint bar, third offense: the first draft lost the Lambert term outright (`diffuse = vis` where the product belonged — an occlusion term *composes into* lighting, never replaces it), returned fractions from an `int` function, and defined the overlap function below `main` (GLSL has no hoisting). The matrix type-checks the Odin half of the interface; the GLSL half's only checkers are the runtime compile log and your own eyes.
  - The milestone landed on the day of the real 2026-08-12 eclipse (partial over Stockholm, magnitude 0.844, maximum 17:56 UTC): with milestone 32's catch-up, a no-args launch at `sim_speed = 1` runs the event in sync with the sky — milestone 31's eclipse *prediction* became a watchable render, and the sim-vs-sky timing offset is the accumulated lunar error, measured by the sky itself. TDB-vs-UTC (~69 s in 2026) is the known floor under any such comparison.

- **Reverse time** (milestone 34) is done: ← below `sim_speed` 1 flips the arrow of time and the two speed keys walk one signed ladder (… −4 −2 −1 +1 +2 +4 …), with the flip at the 1-crossing. The whole feature is an app-side sign: velocity Verlet is a time-reversible map (kick–drift–kick with −DT is the exact inverse of the forward step — milestone 16 quietly bought this), and `physics_step` already took `dt` as a parameter, so `sim_core` changed by zero lines. Rewind is physics run backward from the current state, *not* history replay: merges, deletes, and burns stay done — accepted and by design.
  - Direction is orthogonal state, never a sign smuggled into `sim_speed`: the accumulator stays unsigned time debt, `alpha` stays a valid lerp fraction, and the governor, both clamps, and the overload branch stayed correct without edits — `max(1, speed/2)` slows reverse toward −1 exactly as it slows forward toward +1, for free. Smuggling the sign in would have re-run the representation-migration audit across every read/write/guard site.
  - The zero-value trap, fourth appearance: direction first shipped as `time_dir: int` (±1) omitted from the `state_init` return literal — zero-inits to 0, `dt = 0`, a frozen sim whose arrow keys both *accelerate* (0 matches neither `== +1` nor `== -1`, so both keys fall to their else branches). The fix was representational, not a patched literal: `time_reversed: bool`, whose zero value *is* forward — choose representations whose zero value is the correct default, and the literal-omission trap class can't reach them.
  - Reversal is exact in math, approximate in f64: each backward step inverts its forward twin only to rounding, so forward-then-back lands near the start, not bitwise on it. The acceptance observable is the retrace — the tracked Moon walks its own trail backward, point for point; sideways drift means a missed sign.
  - Forward paths proven untouched the cheap way: deterministic builds never read `time_reversed`, and the 100-step dump stayed bitwise identical to milestone 33's. One `odin check` cell segfaulted once and never again — a transient compiler crash; a matrix failure that won't reproduce is the toolchain, not the tree.

- **Live-sky check** (milestone 35) is done: watched the 2026-08-12 partial eclipse over Sweden beside a no-args launch at `sim_speed = 1` — the sim rendered the eclipse in step with the sky, confirmed by eye. The "debug" half of the milestone went unused: the chain built through milestones 30–33 (real-date specs, barycentric reflex, launch catch-up, the occlusion term) passed its final auditor as shipped.
  - This is the one test with every subsystem in the loop at once — element specs, composition, integrator, catch-up, shadow term, JD/UTC arithmetic — so a pass certifies the *composition*, which no per-part Horizons diff does. Its resolution is coarse (naked-eye timing on a slow partial, with the known TDB-vs-UTC and lunar-phase floors underneath), so it complements the numeric harness rather than retiring it: the eye checks the whole, Horizons checks the parts.

- **Unit tests for the pure leaves** (milestone 36) are done: `date_test.odin` and `body_test.odin` next to the code they test, `odin test opengl/sim/core` appended to check.sh after the 17-cell matrix. The scope is exactly the pure leaves — `date_from_jd`, `kepler_solve`, `spec_rel_state` — because everything stateful already has a better oracle: a unit test of `physics_step` would just be a worse determinism dump. `kepler_solve` and `spec_rel_state` went `@(private = "file")` → `@(private)`; the boundary worth defending was always the *app*, and package-private still defends it.
  - Test files are ordinary package members, so the six core matrix cells lint them under every define for free. Gating them behind `when ODIN_TEST` would have been the false-`when` trap deliberately re-entered — `ODIN_TEST` is set by `odin test` alone, so no `odin check` cell would ever have type-checked the tests. The inverse also holds and bit immediately: `odin test` does *not* run vet, so a dead local left the suite green while the matrix failed. Green tests are not a green matrix.
  - The invariant battery is the design: no expected position vectors anywhere (that's a second transcription of the same math — a change-detector, not a test), just properties any correct elements→state map must satisfy. Vis-viva, energy, and |h| are all *rotation-blind*, so a scrambled p̂/q̂ basis passes every one; the orbit normal and the eccentricity vector are the only witnesses to the rotation, and they're what caught the mutation.
  - Every test was seen failing once, by deliberate mutation before commit — a flipped q̂ sign, a flipped sign in Newton's derivative, a disabled negative-era adjustment. The dead-guard lesson (milestone 27's uniform warnings, the review sweep's `if mv == -1`) says an assertion never observed firing is unverified code; the cure costs ten minutes and is the difference between a suite and a rubber stamp.
  - The first real bug found by anything other than the sky, Horizons, or the eye: `int(day_frac * 1440)` truncated the clock a minute early on **704 of 1440** minute boundaries. A JD near 2.4e6 carries ~20 µs of f64 representation noise (half an ulp), and truncation punishes a negative error of *any* size — magnitude is irrelevant, only the sign is. Two fix attempts missed before the third: rounding `day_frac` collapsed a [0,1) value to 0 or 1, and rounding the minutes breaks clock semantics (a clock reads 19:59 until 20:00 *arrives*) while re-opening a 24:00 rollover. The shape that works nudges the instant once, in days, before the floor — then the day number and the time-of-day derive from one value and cannot disagree, and the fraction stays in [0,1) so hour 24 is unreachable by construction.
  - Two tests that disagree pin a spec no single test can: the midpoint case forbids round-to-nearest, the boundary case forbids bare truncation, and together they specify floor-with-tolerance. Tolerances are *derived*, not guessed — the Kepler round-trip's error scales as dE/dM = 1/(1 − e·cos E), so one flat epsilon would be simultaneously too loose at e=0 and spuriously red at e=0.95.
  - Free measurement from testing past the domain: `E₀ = M` Newton under the 12-iteration cap converges through e = 0.95 and diverges at 0.99 (E came back 57,844). The spec table maxes at ~0.25, so this is margin, not a defect — recorded in the test with `E₀ = π` named as the hardening if a future row ever needs it.

- **Sun bloom** (milestone 37) is done: an HDR post-process chain built in six stages — the frame renders into an `RGBA16F` offscreen target, a bright pass keeps what clears a luminance threshold at half resolution, a separable Gaussian ping-pongs it wide, and the composite adds the halo back, tonemaps, and performs the single gamma encode. Little of it is bloom-specific: the render targets, the shared fullscreen triangle, and the linear-light contract are what milestones 38 and 39 inherit. The star's intensity is derived from geometry rather than taste — scaled by `(radius / draw_radius)²`, the ratio by which the marker clamp exaggerates the Sun's area — so the halo fades as you retreat because received flux really does fall as 1/d². Physics-neutrality proven the usual way: 100-step dump bitwise identical to milestone 36, 17-cell matrix clean.
  - The pipeline's new invariant is one sentence — *everything written into the scene target is linear, and the composite performs the only encode* — and three of the four shader outputs violated it beforehand. The emissive and trail branches had neither decode nor encode and looked correct only because no math ran between them; a pass-through is indistinguishable from a correct round-trip until something in the middle multiplies. Alpha blending moving into linear space is visible and predictable: over black, a trail's screen value goes from `α·c` to `α^(1/2.2)·c`, and `α^(1/2.2) > α`, so the faint tail roughly doubles in brightness. Deriving the *direction* of that change beforehand is what separates "the fix landed" from "something moved".
  - The composite is an ordered chain and each step fails differently out of place: add the bloom, *then* tonemap, *then* encode. Tonemapping before the add lets the added halo climb back above 1 and undoes the compression it just did. ACES (Narkowicz's fit) is not a highlight-only operator — it lifts low and mid tones as well as compressing highs — so adopting it shifts the entire image, planets included. That global shift arrives in the same commit as the bloom and reads as a bug unless it was predicted.
  - The −1 dead-uniform detector from milestone 33 covers exactly one of the two failure directions, because it can only inspect uniforms that were looked up. Declared in Odin, dead in GLSL → the warning fires. Declared in GLSL, absent from Odin → nothing looks, nothing warns, and the uniform silently holds its zero default; `emissive_intensity` cost a black Sun that way. The structural guard is a count rather than a per-item check: `GL_ACTIVE_UNIFORMS` against the number of fields in the load literal turns "did I forget one" into an assertion.
  - Four new silent failures, one theme — these calls take their target from *bound state*, not from their arguments, so moving a bind moves more than it appears to. `TexParameteri` with no texture bound configures the default object and leaves the real one on `NEAREST_MIPMAP_LINEAR`, mipmap-incomplete and therefore an incomplete framebuffer; it appeared the moment the allocation moved into its own proc, because the `BindTexture` above it had been doing two jobs. `BindTexture(TEXTURE0, …)` instead of `TEXTURE_2D` raises `INVALID_ENUM` and binds nothing, leaving whatever was bound before live and sampling it instead. Core profile refuses to draw with VAO 0 bound, so a fullscreen triangle with no vertex data still needs a generated, permanently empty VAO. And an empty fragment shader compiles "successfully" into an object with no `main`, failing one stage later as *"Compiled fragment shader was corrupt"* — a message pointing nowhere near a zero-byte file.
  - A framebuffer carrying only a colour attachment is **complete**, which makes a lost depth attachment invisible to every check in the file. Ordering `GenRenderbuffers` after the storage call meant `RenderbufferStorage` wrote to renderbuffer 0 and `FramebufferRenderbuffer` received a name with no object behind it: the completeness guard passed, the panic never fired, and `DEPTH_TEST` quietly became a no-op. A guard cannot report a failure it isn't positioned to see — only watching a moon pass behind its planet catches this one.
  - The flux factor is the third consumer of the `draw_radius`/`radius` pair from milestone 33, with the same silent inversion if they are crossed. Conserving flux *exactly* turns out to be unusable without exposure adaptation — the Sun renders black by Saturn — so only the headroom above 1.0 scales: `1 + (EMISSIVE_INTENSITY − 1)·flux_scale`. The disc floors at its palette colour and stays visible, and the bloom extinguishes itself because that floor sits below the existing threshold. No zoom branch anywhere, and `EMISSIVE_INTENSITY` stops being a taste knob — it now sets how far from the star the glow survives, which leaves `BLOOM_STRENGTH` as the only cosmetic dial.
  - The halo pulses as the camera moves, and the textbook fix is the wrong one. The Karis average defends an average against a single extreme outlier among dark neighbours; the Sun is a uniform disc of equally hot pixels, so there is no outlier to suppress and applying it would only dim the bloom. The real cause is whole-pixel coverage quantization — a hard-edged ~8-pixel marker with no antialiasing covers a different integer number of pixels at each sub-pixel offset, so total emitted energy jitters every frame. The fix is a rim fade (`smoothstep` on `r²` with `fwidth`), deferred; it needs the 32-segment fan's vertices pushed past radius 1 first, since the polygon edge sits at `r²` between 0.990 and 1.0 and would clip the band.
  - Open, and predating the bloom: at extreme zoom-out the Sun reappears at full scale on some zoom steps. Confirmed still present at `BLOOM_STRENGTH = 0`, so it is geometry and the bloom only made it obvious. Two suspects sit in the camera — `camera_dolly` clamps `distance` at neither end, and `near`/`far` are `distance × 1e-3` and `× 1e5`, a 10⁸ range no 24-bit depth buffer can resolve.
  - **Correction to the open item above**: the Sun reappearing at full brightness at extreme zoom-out and the halo pulsing were *both* the missing flux term, not geometry. Stage 6 fixed both, confirmed by forcing `flux_scale = 1.0` and watching the artifact return. The wrong diagnosis came from a null test on the wrong knob — `BLOOM_STRENGTH = 0` was run before the flux term existed, and zeroing the composite's final multiplier says nothing about the Sun still being written into the scene buffer at 8× intensity at every distance. A null result on one knob exonerates that knob, not the subsystem it belongs to.

- **Spheres** (milestone 38) are done: the billboard fan is a 16×32 UV-sphere mesh (unindexed triangles, poles on model z, position doubling as normal), and the model-view is `translate(center_view) · view · rotate_z(spin) · scale(draw_radius)`, composed in f64 and narrowed once. Depth is real, so a moon now clips against its planet's curved limb and the trail tip disappears inside the body instead of crossing a plane. Spin is a signed sidereal `rotation_period` per spec row; the phase (where the prime meridian points at the epoch) is milestone 39's, with the sub-solar longitude at a pinned date as its auditor. Physics-neutral the usual way: 100-step dump bitwise identical to milestone 37, 17-cell matrix clean.
  - Putting the poles on model z made stage 1 a pixel-identical refactor: with no rotation in the model-view, the sphere is seen pole-on and its equator ring *is* the old 32-segment fan. The same fact is the stage-2 trap — a `vec2` attribute over a three-float buffer silently drops z, so the sphere stays flat with no error anywhere; the only tell is the observable (the trail tip still crossing the body) failing to appear.
  - The normal is `normalize(mat3(mv) · a_pos)` with no inverse transpose, exact because the model-view is rotation times *uniform* scale. That replaces the impostor's "exact because the MV has no rotation"; both die at the first non-uniform scale. The discriminator for a wrong normal transform is the terminator: rotate the mesh under stale normals and the day–night line swings with the camera or the spin.
  - `v_pos_view` is now a genuine surface point and the eclipse reconstruction must still not use it: it sits at the clamped `draw_radius`, so at marker zoom the shadow math would see planets thousands of times too large. `center + N·body_radius` stays — the two-radius rule's would-be fourth consumer, refused.
  - Spin needs the alpha correction, not `sim_time`. One `DT` is 502 s and Jupiter turns 5° per step; at sim speed 1 a step lands every eight real minutes, so spinning on `sim_time` freezes the planet and jerks it. `render_time = sim_time − (1 − alpha)·dt_signed`, computed beside `alpha` from the same accumulator, puts the spin at the instant the positions are drawn at, and the signed `dt` reverses it under reverse time for free.
  - Without obliquity every axis is ecliptic z, so the period's sign is the only orientation a row can express: sense of rotation seen from ecliptic north. Uranus and Pluto are negative (IAU poles below the ecliptic), and their synchronous moons follow their >90° orbits. When obliquity lands, Uranus flips to positive with a 97.8° tilt — five rows to revisit, the Uranus line says so.
  - Traps: an editor completion auto-imported two `core:crypto` packages into `render.odin` and the matrix stopped at the first one; `π·π` for `2π` in the spin rate was invisible in every observable except timing one Jupiter turn against the title-bar date (6.3 h instead of 9.9). `PolygonMode` accepts only `FRONT_AND_BACK` in core profile, is global state, and must be restored after the body pass or the post-process triangle draws as three lines; the wireframe is invisible at marker scale by construction.
  - Deferred: obliquity as a second spec field (never scaled by `INCL_SCALE` — it is orientation, not orbital plane); face culling (winding is counter-clockwise from outside and the fullscreen triangle is counter-clockwise, so it is one line when wanted); milestone 37's rim fade no longer applies as written, since the fan whose vertices it needed pushed past radius 1 is gone.
